Course Structure

The course is divided into six, three-hour sessions. Each session will consist of a mix of lectures, interactive problem-solving, and group work, with advanced problems and concepts specifically aimed at postgraduate-level preparation.

Session 1: Mathematical Foundations

Focus: This session will establish the language of higher mathematics, bridging the gap between procedural calculation and the emphasis on proof and formal reasoning.

 

Topics:

 

Set Theory: Notation, operations (union, intersection, complement), Venn diagrams, power sets, and Cartesian products. Introduction to cardinality.

 

Logic and Proof: Propositional logic, quantifiers (∀, ∃), and methods of proof (direct, contrapositive, contradiction, induction).

 

Functions: Formal definition (domain, codomain, range), properties (injectivity, surjectivity, bijectivity), composition, and inverse functions.

 

Activities:

 

Interactive exercises on set theory and logical notation.

 

Group work on constructing proofs of varying difficulty.

 

PG Focus: Problems involving proofs on the cardinality of sets and properties of functions.

 

 

Session 2: Advanced Algebra and Complex Numbers

Focus: This session will consolidate core algebraic skills and delve into the theory of complex numbers, essential for many areas of advanced mathematics and physics.

 

Topics:

 

Polynomials and Rational Functions: Factor/remainder theorems, roots of polynomials, and decomposition into partial fractions.

 

Complex Numbers: Arithmetic, the Argand diagram, modulus-argument form, Euler's formula (e

iθ

=cosθ+isinθ), and De Moivre's theorem.

 

PG Focus: Introduction to abstract algebraic structures (definition of a group).

 

Activities:

 

Practice problems on partial fractions and polynomial equations.

 

Exercises using Euler's formula and De Moivre's theorem to solve equations and prove trigonometric identities.

 

PG Focus: Identifying whether simple sets with a binary operation form a group.

ession 3: Calculus I - Differentiation and Series

Focus: This session will deepen the understanding of differentiation and introduce the concept of approximating functions with infinite series.

 

Topics:

 

Limits and Continuity: An intuitive introduction, leading to differentiation from first principles.

 

Advanced Differentiation: Chain, product, and quotient rules; implicit and parametric differentiation.

 

Taylor and Maclaurin Series: Using series to approximate functions.

 

Applications: Optimisation and curve sketching.

 

Activities:

 

Worked examples of advanced differentiation techniques.

 

Finding Taylor series for common functions.

 

PG Focus: Discussion of the formal (ϵ−δ) definition of a limit and conditions for convergence of a series.

Session 4: Calculus II - Integration and Differential Equations

Focus: This session will cover a range of integration techniques and their application to solving differential equations.

 

Topics:

 

Advanced Integration Techniques: Integration by substitution and by parts.

 

Differential Equations: Solving first-order separable and linear differential equations.

 

PG Focus: Introduction to second-order homogeneous differential equations and an overview of multivariable calculus (partial derivatives and gradients).

 

Activities:

 

Practice problems on a variety of integration techniques.

 

Solving real-world problems using first-order differential equations.

 

PG Focus: Solving simple second-order differential equations and calculating partial derivatives of functions of two variables.

Session 5: Linear Algebra

Focus: This session will introduce the fundamental concepts of linear algebra, a cornerstone of modern mathematics, data science, and computational modelling.

 

Topics:

 

Vectors, Matrices, and Determinants: A review of arithmetic, scalar (dot) product, and vector (cross) product.

 

Systems of Linear Equations: Solving systems using Gaussian elimination.

 

PG Focus: Introduction to abstract vector spaces, basis, dimension, and the concepts of eigenvalues and eigenvectors.

 

Activities:

 

Exercises on vector manipulation and matrix calculations.

 

Solving systems of linear equations.

 

PG Focus: Finding the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of 2x2 and 3x3 matrices and determining if a set of vectors forms a basis for a vector space.

Session 6: Probability and Statistics

Focus: This session will provide a solid foundation in probability and statistics, essential for degrees in mathematics, statistics, data science, and quantitative finance.

 

Topics:

 

Probability Theory: Set-based probability, conditional probability, and Bayes' Theorem.

 

Random Variables: Discrete (Binomial, Poisson) and continuous (Normal, Exponential) probability distributions; expectation and variance.

 

Hypothesis Testing: The fundamental concepts and terminology.

 

Activities:

 

Problem-solving tasks using Bayes' Theorem and probability distributions.

 

A guided introduction to conducting a simple hypothesis test.

 

PG Focus: Introduction to probability density functions (PDFs) and cumulative distribution functions (CDFs) for continuous random variables.
